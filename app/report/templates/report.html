Off-Page Action Plan

Off-page SEO takes months, not days. Here are the most effective things to focus on:

1. Get listed in local and niche directories
Submit your site to relevant directories — Google Business Profile, Yelp, industry associations, local chambers of commerce. These are easy, free backlinks that also help local SEO.
2. Write guest posts for other sites in your niche
Reach out to blogs or news sites in your industry and offer to write an article. Include a link back to your site. One good guest post on a high-authority site is worth more than 50 low-quality links.
3. Get mentioned in local press and media
Local newspaper sites, community blogs, and event listings often link to businesses they cover. Sponsor a local event, issue a press release, or reach out to local journalists.
4. Ask suppliers and partners to link to you
If you work with suppliers, wholesalers, or partner businesses, ask them to add your site to their "stockists" or "partners" page. These are among the easiest legitimate backlinks to get.
5. Create content worth linking to
Guides, data, original research, tools, and useful resources attract natural links over time. "Ultimate guides" and local area pages tend to earn links in local SEO.
6. Be active on social media
Social signals don't directly affect rankings, but an active social presence drives traffic, brand searches, and indirectly helps earn links. Post consistently on the platforms where your audience is.
7. Fix or reclaim lost links
Use a tool like Ahrefs or Google Search Console to find links pointing to broken pages on your site. Redirect those pages to the correct live page so you don't lose the link value.

How we check your site

What does "On-site" cover?
Everything on your own website — page titles, descriptions, headings, images, page speed, and code quality. These are things you (or your developer) can fix directly.
What does "Indexing" mean?
Whether Google can find and read your pages. We check your sitemap, robots.txt file, broken pages, redirects, and duplicate content.
What is "Linking & Social"?
Other websites linking to yours (backlinks) and your social media presence. This is the hardest area to improve — it takes time and outreach.
Where does the backlink data come from?
We use Common Crawl, a free public database of the web. It gives a partial picture — for a full backlink report, tools like Ahrefs or Semrush are needed.
What is Domain Authority?
A score that predicts how well your site ranks. It requires paid tools (Moz, Ahrefs) so we can't show it here.
What is AI Readiness?
How well your content is structured for AI tools like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews. We check for Schema markup, FAQ sections, and clear question-and-answer formats.
What is Structured Data (Schema)?
Hidden code that tells Google what your page is about. Good Schema can earn you star ratings or FAQ answers shown directly in Google search results.
